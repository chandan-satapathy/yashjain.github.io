# Life in Consulting: A Meme Review

Four years in consulting taught me many things. How to build a deck at 2am. How to find the "so what" in a 40-slide document that has no "so what." How to smile on a client call while your laptop is actively dying.

But the most important thing it taught me is that consultants, more than any other profession, have been *thoroughly* memed. And honestly? Fair.

Here's my honest review of the best consulting memes on the internet.

---

## 1. The Deck Review Cycle

![This is fine](https://i.imgflip.com/9vct.jpg)

This is me at 11pm the night before a client presentation, reading my partner's feedback email that says *"Good start, let's simplify."*

The deck has 48 slides. It started with 30. We simplified twice already.

This is fine. Everything is fine.

---

## 2. The 80/20 Rule, In Practice

![Distracted boyfriend](https://i.imgflip.com/1ur9b0.jpg)

**Me** → *Distracted boyfriend*
**Sleeping** → *The woman he's with*
**Making one more change to the model at midnight** → *The woman he's looking at*

The 80/20 rule is the most quoted and least followed principle in consulting. We say "good enough is good enough." Then we rebuild the entire Excel from scratch because a label was off-centre.

---

## 3. Client Feedback, Translated

![Drake approves disapproves](https://i.imgflip.com/30b1gx.jpg)

**Drake no:** "We'd like you to challenge our thinking."

**Drake yes:** "We'd like you to confirm what we already decided and present it back to us in a nice font."

This is not cynicism. This is a service.

---

## 4. The Pyramid Principle

![One does not simply](https://i.imgflip.com/1bij.jpg)

One does not simply open a Word document and start writing from the beginning.

You must start with the conclusion. Then structure three supporting arguments. Then find data that supports the arguments you already have. This is called *hypothesis-led thinking* and it is a skill that will both save your career and ruin your ability to enjoy novels.

---

## 5. Deliverables Week

![Two buttons](https://i.imgflip.com/1zun7p.jpg)

**Button 1:** Sleep
**Button 2:** Add one more slide because "the narrative isn't tight enough yet"

*Sweating profusely.*

Every. Single. Time.

---

## In All Seriousness

Joking aside — I genuinely love the work. There's something uniquely satisfying about walking into a room with a problem you didn't create, with a team you just met, and leaving with a plan that actually moves things forward.

The memes are the price of admission. Worth it.
